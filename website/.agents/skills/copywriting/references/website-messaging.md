# Messaging before an existing-site redesign

Use when copying, rebuilding or substantially redesigning an existing website
and deciding how much its message may change. A narrow visual edit, exact-copy
instruction or technical migration does not need a new messaging interview.

## Choose the content path first

Read the brief and inspect the existing homepage and relevant service/product
content before asking. If the source cannot be read, disclose the gap and use
supplied content; do not invent an audit. Before visual research, comps or copy
rewrites, resolve this choice with one focused question unless already settled:

"Should we redesign with the exact current copy, or first rethink the messaging
and content structure around the visitor's needs and main call to action?"

- **Keep current copy:** retain the wording, headings, factual content and CTA
  labels. Improve their visual presentation and grouping; preserve meaningful
  reading order, and do not silently shorten, delete or rewrite text to fit a
  layout. A strict replica also preserves whatever structure the brief pins.
- **Rethink messaging first:** reshape the opening, narrative, section sequence
  and wording around the visitor's situation, desired result and real next step.
  Retain the site's substantive information and commercial qualifications while
  making it easier to understand and act on.

Explain the practical tradeoff: the first limits editorial change; the second
allows a stronger customer-focused argument and needs a short messaging review.
Recommend based on the inspected content and brief. A business-description-led
site may benefit from the second path; do not claim that rewriting guarantees
more conversions. Wait for the choice before dependent work. A request to
"redesign" or "copy this site" alone does not choose the second path. Explicit
instructions to retain copy or improve messaging already settle it. If judgment
is delegated, state the chosen path and basis instead of reopening the choice.

Record the path in existing task notes or the surface brief so later designers
and reviewers use the same content authority. Independent source inventory can
continue while the answer is pending.

## Keep the substance traceable

Inventory the pages and content within the agreed scope. Preserve an accessible
source baseline, then track where the useful information will live in the new
site. Scale the record to the task: source page/section, important information,
destination, and whether wording stays exact, is rewritten or is consolidated.
This can live in existing working notes; it does not require a separate system.

Keep service/product coverage, service areas, contact details, hours, prices,
eligibility, limitations, legal terms, qualifications and attributed proof.
Consolidating repeated statements is different from dropping unique information.
Move supporting detail to an appropriate findable section or page when in scope;
leaving it only in a private archive does not preserve it for visitors. Identify
proposed omissions and get agreement before removing substantive content. Do not
replace detailed service information with a vague benefit headline.

Existing claims still need their original qualifications and evidence. Preserve
uncertainty or flag a conflict; do not amplify an unverified claim. Keep real
testimonials verbatim with attribution. Preserve URLs and working destinations
unless changes are in scope, and follow the site's migration workflow for any
authorized route changes.

## Rethink the message around the visitor

Use a loose StoryBrand-inspired lens: the visitor has a need and a desired
outcome; the business helps them reach it. This guides the argument, not a fixed
page template or mandatory seven-section sequence. Establish:

- Who arrives, what is happening in their life or work, and what they need next.
- The useful outcome the actual service or product supports, stated plainly.
- How the business helps, with available evidence of competence or fit.
- The real process or simple next steps, without inventing how the business works.
- The main CTA and its destination, matching the visitor's readiness and intent.

Lead with the customer's situation or result when useful, then explain the offer
clearly enough that the visitor still knows what the business does and where.
Connect service details to those needs rather than deleting the service list.
Keep company history and technical detail where they help a decision. Address
real objections with supplied facts; do not manufacture fear, urgency, guarantees
or failure scenarios to complete a storytelling formula.

For illustration, a site offering leak repair could move from "We are a plumbing
company. Here are our services" toward "Get that leak fixed and get back to your
day," supported by the actual repair services and a working call/contact action.
That reframes the emphasis; it does not establish same-day availability, fixed
pricing or a guarantee. Use the business's own voice rather than copying this line.

## Let the message lead the visual brief

For the messaging path, show a compact proposal before detailed visual directions:
the central message, draft opening headline and support, primary CTA, proposed
section sequence, and where existing detail is retained. Make material changes of
emphasis visible beside the source. Settle consequential framing with the user
as part of the initial brief, or honor delegated direction. This is not a request
to approve every sentence or run a second full design tournament.

Carry that content baseline into reference selection, art direction and comps.
Choose image subjects, type hierarchy and section compositions that communicate
the argument. Maintain enough real copy to judge whether the design accommodates
it; do not let a comp generator invent the message. An approved rewrite becomes
the baseline for preservation and fidelity checks, so later review does not
silently restore the discarded company-first framing.

Before handoff, compare against the source inventory for missing facts, changed
conditions and broken destinations. On the exact-copy path, check wording as
well as presence. On the messaging path, verify that unique information remains
findable, the offer is understandable, and the CTA performs the promised next
step. Report unresolved content gaps; visual approval does not authorize factual
changes, publication or deployment.
